Headline Detective

i

Why headlines lie a little (or a lot)

Headlines are written to make you click — often by an editor who never wrote the actual article. Words like "destroys," "secret," or "shocking" can turn an ordinary update into something that sounds urgent or dramatic.

This matters because most people only read the headline, not the article. If the headline overstates the story, that's the version of reality that sticks in your head.

Emotion Detector

i

Why your feelings are the target

Content that makes you feel something — fear, urgency, envy, guilt — is far more likely to be shared, clicked, or acted on than content that simply informs you. That's not an accident; it's often the whole design.

This matters because decisions made from a strong emotional reaction (buy now, share now, donate now) are rarely as well-considered as decisions made calmly.
