The Roast — Profile Report Card

What a recruiter actually thinks in the first 3 seconds. No sugarcoating.

⚠ Biggest finding Your LinkedIn export shows a Summary and Education section — but no Experience section at all. You have 5 real internship certificates. A recruiter scrolling your profile right now sees zero proof of any of them. This alone is probably costing you more interviews than anything else on this page.

1. Headline

6/10
"Okay, a student who wants to be a frontend dev. Next profile."
❌ The problem: "Frontend Developer | CS Student | Aspiring MERN Stack Developer" — the word Aspiring quietly tells the recruiter "not qualified yet." It's honest, but it undersells you.
🧠 Why it hurts: Recruiters skim headlines to decide search relevance. "Aspiring" doesn't match search filters like "React Developer" or "Frontend Developer" — it reads as a hedge, not a title.
🔍 Invisible cost: You don't show up as confidently in recruiter keyword searches, and the headline gives zero proof (no tech stack proof, no differentiator).

2. About — First 2 Lines (the hook)

4/10
"Another 'I'm a dedicated CS student' opener. *scrolls past*"
❌ The problem: "I'm Sadia Ramzan, a dedicated Computer Science student with a strong interest in both web development and graphic design." LinkedIn already shows your name and headline right above this — repeating it wastes your most valuable real estate.
🧠 Why it hurts: The first 2 lines are the ONLY thing visible before "see more." If they don't earn a click, the rest of your (actually good) story never gets read.
🔍 Invisible cost: Recruiters bounce before reaching your real skills and internships, which are buried further down.

3. About — Full Section

5/10
"Sweet, earnest, but no proof. Sounds like every other CS student."
❌ The problem: Phrases like "I enjoy turning ideas into..." and "My goal is to gain hands-on experience" are soft and passive. There isn't a single number, project name, or result anywhere in it.
🧠 Why it hurts: Recruiters scan for evidence, not intentions. "I enjoy design" tells them nothing about whether you're good at it.
🔍 Invisible cost: Your 5 internships, your portfolio site, your certificates — none of it is mentioned here. This section undersells everything you've actually built.

4. Experience

2/10
"No experience listed. Moving on to the next candidate."
❌ The problem: Your profile export has no Experience section. Your 3+ internships (SoftGrowTech, DevelopersHub Corporation, Apexcify Technologies) only exist as feed posts with certificate images — not as structured, searchable Experience entries.
🧠 Why it hurts: Recruiters filter and search by the Experience section specifically. Posts disappear from view after a few weeks; Experience entries are permanent and structured.
🔍 Invisible cost: This is your single biggest lost opportunity. Real, verifiable internship experience that isn't costing you anything to add — and it's just... not there.

5. Skills & Keywords

3/10
"Only 3 skills? Is that... it?"
❌ The problem: Your Top Skills section shows only HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript (ES6+) — even though your About mentions React, Bootstrap, Tailwind, and Adobe Illustrator. LinkedIn allows up to 50 skills.
🧠 Why it hurts: LinkedIn's search algorithm heavily weights the Skills section. Fewer skills = fewer keyword matches = you show up in fewer recruiter searches.
🔍 Invisible cost: Recruiters searching "React Developer" or "Tailwind CSS" right now will never find you, even though you use both.

7-Day LinkedIn Activation Plan

Not just edits — actions that actually get you seen.

01MON

Make all profile changes

  • Update headline (use Option 1, keyword-optimized)
  • Replace About section with the rebuilt version above
  • Add all 5 internships as proper Experience entries
  • Expand Skills section to at least 10 skills, pin top 3
02TUE

Publish Post #1 — "I just rebuilt my LinkedIn"

Draft below. Under 1300 characters, hook designed to drive comments.

I just realized my LinkedIn had 5 internships worth of proof... and none of it was actually on my profile.

Just spent the week fixing that — rewrote my About section, added every internship as a proper Experience entry, and cleaned up my skills.

Turns out most of us are sitting on more proof than our profiles show. If you're a fellow CS student — have you actually added your internships as Experience entries, or are they just sitting in your posts like mine were? 👇
03WED

Send 10 strategic connection requests

Target: Frontend/React developers 1-3 years ahead of you, hiring managers at Pakistani dev agencies/startups, recruiters who post "hiring frontend" content, alumni from BZU now in tech.

Hi [Name], I'm a CS student building toward frontend/React roles — really admire the work you're doing at [Company]. Would love to connect and follow your journey.
04THU

Comment on 5 posts in your target space

Use the Value Comment formula: Agree + Add Insight + Ask a Question.

Example: "Totally agree that component reusability is underrated early on — I found the same thing switching from plain CSS to Tailwind, it forced me to think in patterns instead of one-off styles. Curious — do you design the component structure first, or let it emerge as you build?"
05FRI

Publish Post #2 — lessons-learned / hot take

Unpopular opinion: doing 5 internships as a student taught me more about "real" development than any single big project would have.

Each one had different code standards, different team habits, different tools. You stop thinking there's one "right way" to build something — and start noticing patterns across all of them.

If you're a student stacking internships instead of chasing one "perfect" one — you're not behind. You're pattern-matching faster than you think.
06SAT

Engage + expand network

  • Reply to every comment on both posts (even a short reply keeps the post alive in feeds)
  • Send 5 more connection requests using Wednesday's template
07SUN

Review the numbers

Check profile views and connection acceptance rate under "My Network" → "Analytics."

📈 Profile views up sharply: your headline/keyword changes are working — keep the momentum with a Week 2 post.
📉 Connection acceptance below ~30%: your requests are too generic or targeting the wrong people — narrow your target list.
😐 Views flat despite edits: LinkedIn's algorithm rewards activity, not just profile edits — post/comment more, don't just wait.
